# Offer terms — draft addendum

Draft for counsel, 2026-10-03. Do not link this from the in-app terms until a lawyer has reviewed it. The product rules in [terms.md](./terms.md) and [privacy.md](./privacy.md) stay as they are until that review. Dispute terms, arbitration, and a liability cap are omitted on purpose so this draft does not invent a posture the current terms do not have.

This addendum covers posted credit offers. It does not cover the bank-link plan.

## What the offers are

The app can compare a debt you already entered with credit terms, either terms you type or terms copied from an issuer’s public page and dated. The result is a planning estimate of the hired date, the interest, and the fee. It is not a loan, not a line of credit, and not a promise that an issuer will approve you.

The issuer decides approval, the limit, and the rate you actually receive. Their agreement is the one that binds you if you apply. A rate that depends on credit is scored at the highest figure the page discloses. When the page gives only a floor (“as low as”), the app does not rank that offer. You can still type the figures from an offer addressed to you.

We are not a bank, a mortgage broker, or a credit-repair organization. Offers do not use a credit pull, and we do not collect a Social Security number or charge you a fee for an offer. An optional debt import, if you turn it on, has its own consent and is never used to choose or send an offer. We do not link to a mortgage, a home-equity product, or a student-loan refinance. A student loan that already has a forgiveness date is not given replacement terms. You may type a rate for a loan you already owe, including a mortgage you already owe, or a home-equity rate you were quoted to compare against a debt you already owe. That comparison leaves taxes out, and the screen says so.

## Prequalification

If you choose it, you can ask card issuers whether they would likely approve you before you apply. You enter your details on the issuer’s or its partner’s form. The issuer runs its own check, which does not affect your credit score, and tells us which offers and terms it returned. We rank those by your hired date the same way as other offers. Prequalified is the issuer’s word and is not a guarantee. We do not send the issuer your books or anything from a credit import. Declining prequalification does not turn off other offers.

## When an offer appears

Offers stay off until you ask, or until you accept a single question on a card that already qualifies. That question is not shown during setup. If you decline, it stays declined until the balance or the rate on that card changes in the way the product rules describe. You can turn offers off in the app at any time. Turning them off does not change your books or your hired date.

The app shows at most one posted offer per debt. The order is the effect on your hired date, then whether a repair or bill collides with the cash you had free, then leftover cash. We are not paid for a higher place in that order. An offer that costs more than keeping your current debt, that collides with a repair, or that only wins if a harder year is ignored, is not shown as a link. The screen can still say to keep the current debt. Paying the balance down from leftover, when that costs less than the fee, is that sentence. A future repair is not treated as a new balance to finance.

A card you pay in full every month is not offered a balance transfer. A rate the app assumed because you left the APR blank is not used to choose an offer.

## How we are paid

If you leave the app through an offer button and the issuer later approves you, the issuer or its affiliate network may pay us. The screen that contains the button says that, in the same view as the button. The pay does not change which offer is shown.

We do not sell your books. The button does not send your balance, your rate, your ZIP, or your bank login. The issuer receives whatever you later type on their site.

## Your figures

The comparison uses the balance, the APR, and the payment on that debt, plus the fee, the promotional period, and the post-promotional rate on the posted terms. Those figures are ones you typed, or ones your linked bank reported that you confirmed. A rate is never used until you have typed or confirmed it. Bank transactions are used for the budget and to suggest bills or debts that are missing. They are not used to choose an offer. A debt the bank reports as past due is not given an offer.

The estimate assumes you keep paying at least what the plan already uses, and that you do not add new charges to a balance you transferred. A 0% period does not remove the balance. Interest can resume when the period ends if a balance remains. The screen states the fee, the end of the promotional period, and the post-promotional rate used in the math. The comparison ignores any effect on a credit score. It does not use your ZIP.

Posted terms are hidden when the copy we hold is more than 14 days old or the issuer’s date has passed. Terms you type yourself still compare.

## Your choices

You may ignore an offer, dismiss it, or turn the feature off, and the planner continues. Erase everything deletes the on-phone record of offer buttons you tapped, along with the rest of what that action deletes. That record is an offer id and a time. It is not part of the household backup.

You must be 18 or older, the same as the rest of the app.

## Counsel checklist before this is linked from the product

- Affiliate contract pays for a completed application, and does not pay for placement or rank.
- In-app sentence matches the paid-if-approved line above and sits in the same view as the button.
- Privacy text states that offer matching is on device, that books are not sold, and that a click sends no balance.
- Written approval from Plaid for offer matching on rates and balances it supplied, and the express-consent wording shown before offers.
- FTC Safeguards program in place before Plaid Production.
- State credit-services and broker review before any personal-loan or auto-refinance link. Mortgages stay excluded.
- No credit pull, no Social Security number, no “you will be approved,” no use of an assumed APR.
